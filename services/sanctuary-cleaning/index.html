Sanctuary & Worship Space Cleaning in Scandia, MN

Detailed cleaning for the spaces where your congregation gathers to worship — respectful of the environment, reliable on schedule.

The sanctuary is the heart of your building. It is also the space that takes the most visible wear — dusty pew backs, tracked-in carpet soil, fingerprints on polished wood, and the slow accumulation of wax and polish buildup on hard floors. After 22 years of cleaning faith facilities across Minnesota, we understand that sanctuary cleaning requires a different pace and a different level of attention than commercial office cleaning.

What Sanctuary Cleaning Includes

A standard sanctuary visit covers:

  • Pew cleaning — wood surfaces wiped, cushions vacuumed or spot-cleaned as needed
  • Carpet vacuuming along aisles, under pews and in choir areas
  • Aisle runner care (vacuumed and repositioned)
  • Altar and chancel surfaces dusted and wiped
  • Stained glass windowsills and ledges
  • Pulpit and lectern wiped down
  • Entry vestibule swept and mopped
  • Restrooms serving the sanctuary cleaned and restocked

Deep-clean visits (quarterly or as needed) add: pew cushion extraction cleaning, hard floor stripping and waxing, light fixture dusting, and full window sill detail.

Frequency Options

Most Scandia-area congregations choose one of three patterns:

  • Weekly cleaning — typically Friday or Saturday, ahead of Sunday services
  • Bi-weekly cleaning — appropriate for smaller congregations with fewer mid-week events
  • Event-based cleaning — scheduled around weddings, funerals, holiday services and special occasions

If your facility hosts multiple events weekly — a weekday service, a midweek Bible study, a Saturday evening contemporary service — we can schedule light reset visits between the main cleaning.

Who This Suits

Sanctuary cleaning is appropriate for any worship space, from a 150-seat rural church to a multi-room campus. Church administrators and trustees who contact us are usually managing facilities that have outgrown volunteer cleaning — the congregation is willing, but nobody wants to ask a 70-year-old deacon to get on their hands and knees to clean under the pews every Friday.

A professional crew with the right equipment finishes in a fraction of the time and to a consistent standard.
